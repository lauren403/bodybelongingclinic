Where I come in

Three points on the ADHD journey.

While they wait for assessment
Support with what is hard right now, such as routines, emotions, sleep, school or work, and eating, plus help preparing for the assessment so the assessing clinician gets a clearer picture. Anxiety, depression and eating disorders often sit alongside ADHD. If you’ve diagnosed one, a Mental Health Treatment Plan and referral means rebated sessions can begin while they wait. No diagnosis yet? They can start privately.
After diagnosis, alongside medication
The Australian ADHD guideline recommends offering cognitive behavioural interventions to adolescents and adults, and parent and family training for children. I provide that alongside your prescribing, and I tell you what I notice about appetite, sleep and mood along the way.
After diagnosis, without medication
For patients who choose not to take medication, can’t tolerate it, or haven’t started yet, therapy carries the work: practical strategies for attention, follow through and emotion regulation, and help making sense of the diagnosis.

Patients preparing for an assessment can also use the assessment preparation guide in the ADHD library.

Who it’s for

  • Children (with a parent or carer), adolescents and adults with diagnosed ADHD or waiting for an ADHD assessment.
  • People for whom eating, appetite or body image is part of the picture: appetite that drops away on stimulants, skipped meals and evening crashes, binge eating, or a very narrow range of foods.
  • Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander, LGBTQIA+ and neurodivergent patients looking for affirming care.

For children under 10, ADHD assessment, diagnosis and medication sit with a paediatrician, psychiatrist or multidisciplinary team, and I can still see the child and family.

This isn’t a crisis service. Patients at acute risk need an urgent pathway before or alongside a referral here.

What we offer

  • Cognitive behavioural strategies for ADHD after diagnosis, in line with the AADPA guideline, and practical support with what’s hardest while patients wait for assessment, with parents and carers closely involved for children, alongside ACT and DBT skills for values, emotion regulation and distress tolerance.
  • Eating disorder treatment using CBT‑E and family based treatment (FBT), adapted for ADHD, with parents and carers involved for children and adolescents.
  • An eating assessment before you start a stimulant, if you’re not sure whether eating is a concern, with a letter back to you within a week of the session.
  • Screening for disordered eating at the first session, with a clear pathway to an Eating Disorder Plan where you think it’s indicated.
  • Telehealth sessions (video, with phone as a fallback), paced around ADHD attention and follow through.
  • Standard measures for every client at the start and end of each course of sessions, so your reviews have scores to work with. For eating concerns I use the EDE‑Q and CIA, timed to line up with Eating Disorder Plan reviews.

ADHD and eating

When ADHD and eating show up together.

Eating disorders are my main area of practice, and I hold the ANZAED credential. People with ADHD are more likely to develop an eating disorder, and stimulants change appetite, so eating often needs its own attention. Treatment focuses on eating and never aims at weight loss. Where weight restoration is needed for health, it starts early, not when someone feels ready. Weighing and medical targets stay with you. Please send me each weight, weekly while weight is being restored and otherwise at each review, so we can work with it in session. Each report covers progress with eating, and appetite changes on stimulants are reported to you as a clinical signal. If anything suggests medical risk, I contact you straight away, or arrange emergency care if it can’t wait.

Before you start a stimulant

Not sure whether eating is a concern? I’ll assess and write back within a week of the session. If it’s significant, the ADHD side may sit better with a psychiatrist or paediatrician than with the WA ADHD GP Program. I’ll help you make that call early, and stay their therapist either way.

Diagnosis, prescribing, medical monitoring, weighing and growth checks stay with you or the treating specialist. If an eating concern needs a psychiatrist, a paediatrician or more intensive care, I’ll tell you early.

Care is Aboriginal led, neurodivergent affirming and LGBTQIA+ affirming. I’m an Accredited Mental Health Social Worker and ANZAED Credentialed Eating Disorder Clinician, CBT‑E and FBT trained, with around 15 years of clinical experience with children, adolescents and adults. Verify the ANZAED credential.

Referral pathways

Referral pathways we accept.

GP Mental Health Treatment Plan (Better Access)
Up to 10 individual sessions each calendar year, and up to 6 per referral, followed by your review. Since 1 November 2025 the plan, its reviews and the referral must come from a GP at the practice where the patient is enrolled in MyMedicare, or from their usual GP (the GP or practice that has provided most of their care in the past 12 months, or is likely to in the next 12). If you diagnosed the patient and won’t be providing most of their care, please send your report to their usual GP, who can prepare the plan and referral.
Psychiatrist or paediatrician (Better Access)
Refer directly. A Mental Health Treatment Plan isn’t needed, and the usual GP rule doesn’t apply. The referral must follow an attendance item that Medicare lists as eligible for psychiatrists and paediatricians (MBS note MN.6.3). Up to 6 sessions per referral, within the patient’s 10 Better Access sessions each calendar year.
Eating Disorder Treatment and Management Plan
Often called an Eating Disorder Plan. For anorexia nervosa, or bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder or OSFED that meets the MBS criteria: up to 40 eating disorder psychological treatment services in the 12 months after the plan, in courses of 10. A GP, psychiatrist or paediatrician prepares the plan. A review is needed before sessions 11 and 31 (by the managing practitioner where possible), and before session 21 both a GP review and a psychiatrist or paediatrician review. Feeding concerns such as a narrow range of foods, or appetite changes on medication, don’t meet these criteria and usually fit Better Access instead, under the patient’s ADHD or other mental health diagnosis. I provide these services as an eligible social worker for Medicare eating disorder services. The plans, item numbers and review gates →
GP chronic condition management plan
Up to 5 allied health services each calendar year, shared with the patient’s other allied health providers. The service needs to be recommended in the plan, with a signed referral from you. The plan needs to have been prepared or reviewed in the last 18 months; GP Management Plans with Team Care Arrangements made before 1 July 2025 still count until 30 June 2027. The Medicare rebate for these sessions is $63.40.
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health assessment follow up
Up to 10 allied health services each calendar year, shared with the patient’s other allied health providers and including any chronic condition plan sessions, with a signed referral from you, after a GP health assessment (including at an ACCHO) identifies the need, or under a chronic condition management plan. No more than 5 of the 10 can come from a chronic condition plan, and the Medicare rebate for these sessions is $63.40.
NDIS, DVA and WorkCover WA
NDIS participants whose plans are self managed or plan managed (I’m not a registered NDIS provider, so NDIA managed plans can’t be used), DVA clients, and WorkCover WA claims, each with the referral or approval that scheme requires.

Where a patient could use more than one pathway, we’ll talk with you about which one fits. Referral decisions are always yours.

What to include

  • Patient name, date of birth, Medicare number and contact details (and a parent or carer’s details for children)
  • Referral type and date, the number of sessions referred (where applicable), whether a Mental Health Treatment Plan or other plan has been prepared, and a copy where the patient agrees
  • The ADHD diagnosis and who made it and when (or that an assessment is pending), current medication, and any eating concern or diagnosis (with the Eating Disorder Examination Questionnaire score for an Eating Disorder Plan)
  • Relevant history, including any current risk, and anything that would help us make the first session safe and useful
  • Your name, practice, provider number and signature

Use my referral letter template

A one page letter with everything above, already addressed to me. Import it once into your practice software and it’s there for every referral.

Download the template for Best Practice or MedicalDirector (RTF)

The places your software can fill in for you, such as the patient’s details, Medicare number, current medications and your provider number, are marked like «Patient full name». Swap each one for the matching field once, and the template fills itself in from then on. The one page setup guide shows how.

Best Practice: open the word processor, choose Templates, then Import Template, and select the file. Click each marked item, delete it and double click the matching field in the template fields list, because Best Practice won’t recognise a field that is typed in. Tick Available to all users and save.

MedicalDirector: in Letter Writer, choose File, New, then Blank Template (in some versions File, Modify Template, User Defined, then Blank Template). Then choose File, Import, select the file, replace each marked item with the matching field from the Template Fields toolbar, and choose File, Save as Template.

Prefer to fill it in by hand? Download the plain template (RTF).

After session 1

End of each course
Progress, a repeat screen, and whether further sessions are recommended, so you can review and refer again. That is around session 6 under Better Access, and after every 10 sessions under an Eating Disorder Plan.

A summary and suggested next steps: step down, an Eating Disorder Plan, or longer term therapy.
