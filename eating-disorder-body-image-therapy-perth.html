You don’t have to be “sick enough” to deserve care.

A first step

Support doesn’t need to start with certainty.

Recovery here isn’t about willpower, a “before photo”, or making yourself smaller — and it isn’t one-size-fits-all.

You might be looking for eating disorder therapy in Perth, body image support in Nedlands, or online therapy because something about eating, food fear, body checking, shame or recovery has become hard to carry alone.

You may also be trying to find support that makes room for gender, sexuality, culture, neurodivergence, family, kin, safety and social and emotional wellbeing. Body Belonging Clinic was created for people whose story doesn’t fit neatly in one box.

Many people who reach us are navigating ADHD or autism and eating at the same time, where food, focus, interoception, sensory needs and appetite are tangled together. If that’s you, start with ADHD & disordered eating or autism, ARFID & eating.

Worth saying

Care before any threshold

You don’t need a formal diagnosis or a label to deserve support. Disordered eating, food rules and body distress all count — long before they reach a clinical line.

Is this you?

You might recognise some of this.

  • Food has rules, “good” days, “bad” days, fear foods, or a running tally in your head.
  • You check, avoid mirrors, or feel like your body is the problem to fix.
  • Eating can feel secret, shameful, or hard to control.
  • You’re “functioning”, so no one realises how much room food and body take up.
  • You’re not sure it’s “bad enough” to count, and that doubt keeps you stuck.

Any of these? You’re in the right room. You don’t need to be sure, or “sick enough”, to begin.

What support can include

Eating and body work can hold more than food.

Eating disorder therapy

Recovery, formulation & care

Therapy may include assessment, formulation, recovery planning and work with food fear, eating rules, avoidance, body checking, distress, shame and the systems around recovery.

Body image support

Beyond appearance

Body image work may involve noticing what keeps distress going, building practical skills, working with shame, and making room for identity, safety, culture, connection and belonging.

Identity-affirming care

The whole person

Support can be identity-affirming, neurodivergent-affirming, LGBTQIA+ affirming and culturally grounded, with attention to the parts of life that shape how people feel, cope and recover.

Family & referrer involvement

A connected team

Where helpful and consented to, therapy can include family, kin, carers, GPs, dietitians and other referrers so the work around the person is clearer and more connected.

For parents & carers

You don’t have to have the right words.

If your child or teen is struggling with eating, food or their body, you’re likely frightened, and maybe quietly blaming yourself. You didn’t cause this, and you don’t have to fix it alone or say the perfect thing. You are the most important support your young person has, and here you’re part of the team, not left on the outside of it.

We work with children, adolescents and their families where ADHD, autism and eating overlap — sensory needs and safe foods, ARFID, and the appetite changes that come with a young, neurodivergent body — alongside your GP, a dietitian and any medical team where that’s needed. We’ll help you make sense of what’s happening and find a gentle next step together. You deserve support too: Eating Disorders Families Australia and the Butterfly Foundation offer free family and carer resources.

Pathways

Common ways to begin.

There’s no single correct pathway into support. The best first step depends on age, risk, referral needs, Medicare eligibility, current supports and what feels sustainable right now.

01

Book or ask about fit

If you’re ready to begin, book online. If you’re unsure, email the clinic with a brief note about what you’re looking for.

02

Talk with your GP

A GP can help assess health needs and discuss referral pathways such as a Mental Health Treatment Plan or Eating Disorder Treatment and Management Plan.

03

Bring current supports together

Support may involve a GP, dietitian, family, kin, school, workplace or other practitioners — always with your consent, and only where it helps.

04

Start with the whole picture

Early sessions focus on understanding what’s happening around eating, body, identity, safety, culture, connection and wellbeing.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Do I need a diagnosis or referral?

No. You can book privately without one. To access Medicare rebates you’ll need a GP Mental Health Treatment Plan, an Eating Disorder Treatment and Management Plan, or a psychiatrist or paediatrician referral.

What if I’m not sure it’s “an eating disorder”?

You don’t need to be sure to begin. We work with disordered eating, food fear and body distress wherever it sits. You don’t have to have it all worked out first.

Do you work with ADHD and eating together?

Yes. That overlap is a core focus here. If it’s part of your story, start with ADHD & disordered eating.

Safety note

This is not a crisis service.

Body Belonging Clinic is not an emergency or crisis service. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 000. For 24/7 support: the Butterfly Foundation 1800 33 4673 (eating disorders), Lifeline 13 11 14, 13YARN 13 92 76, Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800, or QLife 1800 184 527.
