What this is

A letter that reflects you, in your own words.

Many surgeons ask for a letter from a mental health professional before top surgery or gender-affirming surgery — you may have heard it called a ‘WPATH letter’. What that letter really is: a psychosocial readiness assessment — a considered note that you understand the surgery, have thought it through in your own time, and have support around you. That’s it. It isn’t a test, and it isn’t a verdict on who you are.

My assessment is informed by the WPATH Standards of Care (SOC-8) and AusPATH, and grounded in Australia’s informed-consent model. That approach — affirming, informed-consent, no gatekeeping — isn’t only how I work; it’s where Australian best practice now sits. In practice that usually means one letter for chest or top surgery — but your surgeon’s requirements are the ones we follow. Bring what they’ve asked for, and we’ll match it exactly.

How it works

The whole path, no surprises.

Four gentle steps, at a pace you set. Nothing has to be figured out before you begin.

01

A free welcome session

We meet first, with no pressure and no cost. I hear what you’re holding, we map out how to approach the letter, and you get a sense of whether working together feels right. If you have your surgeon’s requirements or letter template, bring them — we’ll match them exactly. Video or phone, camera never required.

02

The assessment

A single, unhurried 90-minute session to get to know you and your story, in your own words — with a short follow-up only if your situation needs it. We’ll go through informed consent together, so you always know what’s happening and why.

03

Your letter, written with you

I write the psychosocial readiness letter to your surgeon’s requirements. You see it before it goes anywhere — it should sound like you, and feel true. Many surgeons want the letter dated within about three months of surgery, so we’ll time it to suit yours.

04

Ongoing support, if you want it

Some people just need the letter. Others want a steady room for the whole journey — body, identity, family, the lot. Either is right. Ongoing therapy can attract Medicare rebates.

Straight talk, because you deserve it

What the letter is, and what it isn’t.

Being honest with you up front is part of being safe:

  • It’s a readiness assessment, not a medical clearance. Your medical, anaesthetic and nutritional fitness for surgery are for your surgical and medical team — the letter doesn’t speak to those.
  • Your surgeon’s rules govern. SOC-8 generally points to one letter for chest or top surgery, but requirements vary by surgeon and procedure. Bring theirs and we’ll follow them.
  • It’s not a gate. I write with you, reflecting you. No proving your gender, no hoops.
  • A couple of things I don’t do. A statement to change the sex or gender marker on a WA birth certificate must come from a doctor or psychologist registered with AHPRA, so I’ll point you to the right person. And hormone-therapy letters usually aren’t required under Australia’s informed-consent model.
  • No promises in advance. The assessment is genuine, so I can’t promise a letter or a timeline before we’ve met. What I can promise is honesty, warmth, and no surprises.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Do you gatekeep?

No. The letter is written with you and in your own words, not about you. You don’t have to prove your gender or perform anything. My role is to understand you and support you, not to stand between you and your surgery.

How many sessions before the letter?

Usually just one: a single, unhurried 90-minute assessment after the free welcome session, with the letter written to match what your surgeon requires. Occasionally a short follow-up where your situation needs it, and we decide that together.

Is the letter guaranteed?

I can’t promise a letter, an outcome or a timeline in advance, because the assessment is genuine. In practice, most people who come for this are ready. If anything suggests a different next step would serve you better, I’ll be honest and help you find the way forward.

Which surgeons and requirements do you follow?

Your surgeon’s requirements are the ones we follow — bring what they’ve asked for and we’ll match it. My assessment is informed by WPATH Standards of Care (SOC-8) and AusPATH, which generally point to one psychosocial readiness letter for chest or top surgery, but the surgeon’s rules govern.

How much does it cost, and can I use Medicare?

The welcome session is free. The assessment and letter are a private service (no Medicare rebate). It is a single 90-minute session for $450, letter included, with concessions available; occasionally a short follow-up where your situation needs it. Ongoing therapy, if you’d like it, is $200 a session, with Medicare rebates for eligible clients who have a valid GP plan.

Do you write hormone-therapy letters?

Under Australia’s informed-consent model, a mental health letter for hormone therapy usually isn’t required. If your prescriber asks for one, we can talk it through.

Do you write birth-certificate letters?

No. A statement to change the sex or gender marker on a WA birth certificate must come from a doctor or psychologist registered with AHPRA, so that isn’t something I provide — but I’ll happily point you toward the right person.

In person or online?

Online for now — by telehealth from anywhere in Western Australia and across Australia. The rooms in Nedlands are the practice base.
